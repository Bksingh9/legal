## What a legal notice is

A legal notice is a formal written communication sent to a counter-party that records a grievance, frames the legal basis for it, and demands a specific action within a stated deadline. It is the typical first step before a civil suit or a complaint to a tribunal.

## When a legal notice is required

For a handful of specific causes of action, sending a notice is mandatory. Section 80 of the Code of Civil Procedure requires two months' notice before suing the Central or State Government. Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act requires a notice within thirty days of dishonour of a cheque. Many state rent control statutes require notice before eviction. For most other civil disputes a notice is optional but recommended.

## What a notice should contain

A serviceable notice has these elements: the sender's full name and address; the recipient's full name and address; a chronological recital of the relevant facts with dates and places; a statement of the legal basis (the Act and section under which the conduct is actionable); a clear demand for action; a stated deadline counted from the date of receipt; and a closing paragraph reserving the sender's rights to escalate to court at the recipient's risk as to costs.

## The process

1. Gather every document related to the dispute: contracts, invoices, prior correspondence, bank statements, and identity proof of both parties.
2. Reduce the facts to a one-page chronological summary. Be specific about dates and amounts.
3. Decide the demand and the deadline. Be reasonable and specific.
4. Engage an advocate to draft on letterhead. The advocate will issue the notice in their name on instructions from you.
5. Send by registered post acknowledgment due (RPAD) and additionally by email. Keep the postal receipts and the email logs.
6. Track the deadline. If there is no satisfactory response by the stated date, your advocate can initiate civil or criminal proceedings.

## What happens after the deadline

If the recipient ignores the notice, you proceed to file the suit or complaint as outlined in the notice. If the recipient replies, the reply often opens a window to negotiate and settle. If the recipient denies the allegations, the reply itself becomes evidence in any subsequent litigation.
