## What the Act covers

The Consumer Protection Act 2019 protects buyers of goods and services from unfair trade practices, defective goods, deficient services, and misleading advertisements. The Act applies whether the transaction was online or offline, and includes telemedicine, e-commerce, and ride-hailing.

## Forum selection

You file your complaint in the forum whose pecuniary jurisdiction covers the value of the goods or services together with any compensation you claim. The 2021 amendment set the slabs at fifty lakh and two crore rupees for district and state forums respectively, with the NCDRC covering anything above. Territorial jurisdiction follows where the cause of action arose, where the opposite party resides, or where the complainant resides.

## The Section 35 framework

Section 35 of the Act lays out who can file a complaint: the consumer, a recognised consumer association, or the government. The complaint must contain the parties, the facts, the cause of action, and the relief sought. Most district commissions accept a typed complaint on plain paper with supporting documents annexed.

## Reliefs you can ask for

A typical prayer includes refund of the amount paid, replacement of the goods or service, repair where appropriate, compensation for mental agony and inconvenience, interest on the amount from the date of cause of action, and costs of the proceedings. The forum has wide discretion to grant any combination.

## Filing fees

Fees scale by the pecuniary jurisdiction. District commissions charge nominal amounts (often free up to one lakh and a few hundred rupees up to fifty lakh). State commissions charge more, and the NCDRC has its own schedule. Confirm the fee on the forum's website before paying.

## Process after filing

The forum issues notice to the opposite party, who has thirty days to respond. After pleadings and affidavit evidence, the forum may direct mediation or proceed to oral arguments. The Act mandates disposal within five months from the date of receipt of notice if no expert evidence is required, though in practice timelines run longer.

## Common drafting mistakes to avoid

Consumers often understate the cause of action by leaving out the date and the exact amount paid. Vague reliefs are routinely declined. Annexures should be paginated and cross-referenced from the body of the complaint. The verification clause at the end is mandatory; commissions reject defective verifications.
